The Product Management Map

Don’t ask what a PM is supposed to do. Ask where you are on the map, and whether you like it there.

PM archetypes

Some PMs have a founder mindset. They take on the whole product and do everything, from building to growth to data. Others pick the archetype that fits their interests and go deep in it. Both are PMs. Watch how the work splits as a company grows.

Which of these sounds like you?

Many archetypes sit under the PM title. You can grow into several of them if you want to, and you can also pick one and stay there for a whole career.

Who decides what gets built?

This question separates product management from other jobs that share the title.

Code is the new language of product work

English used to be a PM’s primary language. You wrote a PRD, and engineering made it real.

Today, code is the superpower that can put you ahead of other PMs. You don’t need to become an engineer. Learn enough to build, test, and make your ideas tangible. You’ll validate faster, ship sooner, and stop waiting for someone else to get started.

Build

You build screens and flows yourself. A rough prototype in a weekend. Good enough to show, sharp enough to change the conversation.

Growth

You write SQL. You pull your own numbers from Mixpanel or Amplitude. You don't wait for a data analyst to tell you what the funnel looks like.

Data

You live in notebooks. You query, you transform, you visualise. The insight is yours before it reaches anyone else.

Technical

You read the diff. You understand what shipped, what changed, and what the trade-off was. You can shape architecture because you speak the language it is written in.

Platform

You design APIs the way a writer designs a sentence. You know what a good contract looks like before any code is written.

AI

You run evals. You write prompts as specifications. You test models the way others test features.

Ship today

Start a GitHub repository

Put something in it. A prompt, a script, a prototype. The habit of shipping something you made matters more than what it is.

Never written a line of code?

The easiest way to start is to build something small and keep improving it. Open Claude, paste the prompt below, and see what comes back. Then change one thing. Then another. That loop is the whole lesson.

Build me a simple word-guessing game. The player gets 6 attempts to guess a 5-letter word, and after each guess show which letters are correct, which are in the word but in the wrong place, and which are not in the word at all. Make it playable in a browser.

Once it works, ask Claude to add a timer, a score, a hard mode. Every change you request is a conversation in the language you are learning.

This map is a guide, not a rulebook. PM archetypes overlap, and your work shifts with company stage, team size, product maturity, and the people around you.

Moving to a larger company doesn’t always mean career growth or more enjoyable work. What matters is whether you enjoy the work that fills most of your day.
